Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 24, 2026

SaveMe is a free, open-source Windows app that backs up your PC game saves to storage you choose. This policy explains what data SaveMe handles, where that data goes, and your rights under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

  • SaveMe has no servers and no user accounts. The publisher never receives your game saves, files, sign-in details or any other personal data.
  • Your backups go straight from your PC to your own Google Drive, OneDrive or a folder you pick.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or cookies, in the app or on this website.
  • On Google Drive, SaveMe can only see the files it created.

Who publishes SaveMe

SaveMe is published by calinadrian, an individual developer based in Romania (“we”). Contact: hassian.inbox@gmail.com. For bugs and questions that don't involve personal details, you can also use GitHub issues.

What SaveMe handles on your PC

All of this happens on your own computer, under your control, to provide the backups you set up. We don't receive any of it.

Data Why Where it's kept
Game save files To back them up and restore them Zipped and uploaded to the storage you chose
Your cloud account's email and storage quota To show which account is connected and how much space is free Only on your PC
Sign-in tokens for Google or Microsoft To reach your storage without asking you to sign in each time Only on your PC, encrypted with Windows data protection
Your PC's name and a random ID for this installation To show which PC a save came from, and to stop two PCs overwriting each other With each backup, in your storage
The list of running programs To notice when a game closes so it can be backed up Checked in memory, never stored or sent anywhere
Installed games, save locations, backup history and settings To run the app Only on your PC, in %APPDATA%\SaveMe

Backups are ordinary zip files. SaveMe doesn't encrypt them, so they are as private as the storage account or folder you keep them in. Use a strong password and two-step verification for your cloud account.

Services SaveMe connects to

SaveMe talks directly to the services below. Each is responsible for the data it receives under its own privacy policy; we don't receive a copy.

  • Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive, if you connect one: your backups are stored in your account. See the Google Privacy Policy and the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • GitHub (owned by Microsoft): SaveMe downloads the public list of game save locations (the Ludusavi manifest), checks GitHub Releases for new versions on launch and every 6 hours (you can turn this off in Settings), and downloads updates from there. This website is also hosted on GitHub Pages. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Steam Store and image servers (Valve): SaveMe sends a game's Steam app ID to request its current cover artwork. Covers are cached on your PC for offline use. No save files, folder paths, game names or account details are included in these requests.

As with any internet request, these services see your IP address and basic technical details such as the app's user agent. No other personal data is sent with the GitHub and Steam requests. Some of these companies process data outside the European Economic Area under their own safeguards, such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

Google user data

SaveMe asks only for the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file permission. It uses it to create, read, update and delete its own backup files in the folder you choose in your Google Drive (SaveMe by default), and to read your account email and storage quota. It can't see any other files in your Drive.

SaveMe's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. This data stays between your PC and your Google account: it is never sent to us or anyone else, never used for advertising, and never used to train AI or machine-learning models.

Microsoft OneDrive

SaveMe asks only for Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder, User.Read and offline_access. It works only inside the Apps/SaveMe folder of your OneDrive and reads your account name and storage quota.

When you contact us

If you email us or open a GitHub issue, we use what you send (such as your email address and message) only to answer you. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in supporting the people who use SaveMe (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We delete emails when they're no longer needed, at the latest two years after the conversation ends. GitHub issues are public, so don't post personal details there.

This website

This website sets no cookies and uses no analytics, ads, embedded content or external fonts. It's hosted on GitHub Pages, which may log visitors' IP addresses for security, as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Removing your data

  • Stop backups: choose Disconnect in SaveMe's Settings. This deletes the sign-in tokens from your PC.
  • Withdraw access: remove SaveMe at myaccount.google.com/permissions or account.live.com/consent/Manage.
  • Local data: uninstalling SaveMe deletes %APPDATA%\SaveMe. Updates keep it.
  • Backups: they stay in your storage until you delete the backup folder there yourself.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct and delete your personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to data portability. Because SaveMe doesn't send us your data, we usually hold nothing about you beyond any messages you've sent us; to exercise your rights for those, email us and we'll reply within one month. For data held by Google, Microsoft or GitHub, contact them directly.

You can also complain to a data protection authority. In Romania that's the Autoritatea Națională de Supraveghere a Prelucrării Datelor cu Caracter Personal (ANSPDCP), Bd. G-ral. Gheorghe Magheru 28-30, Sector 1, 010336 București, anspdcp@dataprotection.ro. You can also contact the authority in the EU country where you live or work.

Children

SaveMe isn't directed at children and collects no data from anyone. To connect a cloud account, you need to meet that service's own age requirements.

Changes

We'll post any changes on this page and update the date above. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the release notes of the version that brings them.
